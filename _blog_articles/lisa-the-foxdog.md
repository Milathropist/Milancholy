<p style="text-align:justify;"><sup>8th August 2026</sup><br>
Near the end of January 2026, Lisa had a&nbsp;very bad day and the everyone was worried it'd be her last. But she managed to recover. But her health, which was already not great at that time, still went on to slowly decline and fade away. Today, on August 7th, she is having a&nbsp;worse day again. While I&nbsp;do believe she will get better still, I&nbsp;want to write about her while she is still here, rather than wait for her to be gone. <br>
Her mother, Sara, who was mixed with a&nbsp;hunting dog (as in, one of her parents was a&nbsp;hunting dog) once ran away and came back a&nbsp;few days later. This was really the only time she ran away, and it's also the time when she got pregnant. That's how, after some time, Lisa came to be. Her name came from the color of her fur, which looked a&nbsp;bit like fox's fur. Lisa, from the word "lis", which is the Polish word for "fox". It was in the summer of 2012, and young Mila was about 5 years old or so, that's how long ago it was^^. As expected of a&nbsp;dog pregnancy however, Lisa had some siblings. Unfortunately, we couldn't keep everyone, so they were given away to hopefully nice homes where they had or still have happy lives. One of her siblings, the only other pet we named: Luna, was a&nbsp;dark-furred dog, who I&nbsp;also really wanted to keep. My family though didn't want to keep 2&nbsp;dogs, or maybe they just couldn't, but one day my grandpa took my older brothers and Luna claiming they are going to the vet, but they returned without her and only then did they tell me they gave her away. It just so happened that she was apparently the only one to run away from her new home and never come back to it. Maybe it was a&nbsp;mixture of that fact and me not ever being able to properly say goodbye to her that I&nbsp;believe I&nbsp;got to see her 1 last time years later: while waiting for the bus I&nbsp;spotted a&nbsp;dog that reminded me of her. The dog also seemed to notice me and so she came up to me. I&nbsp;let her sniff me and pet her once, after that she left. Maybe it was just a&nbsp;coincidence, and someone's dog ran away and happened to bump into me, noticing the smell of my pets, but I&nbsp;want to believe it was her, that it was Luna, giving me a&nbsp;chance to say goodbye. <br>
The star of today's article however stayed with us like I&nbsp;mentioned. She was initially being taken care of by my older brother, but he was pretty lazy and neglectful about it. Lisa would just stay in her cage, which wasn't small, but wasn't really big either (imagine a&nbsp;dog on a&nbsp;chain for comparison), without being let out for a&nbsp;month or 2 even. She was also mostly fed the food that just wasn't finished by any of the humans (my family or&nbsp;I). It was only after Mila grew up a&nbsp;little that she on her own decided to take care of Lisa from now on. This is when some real improvements started happening. First of all, since I&nbsp;was going to her everyday and all, I&nbsp;started letting her out everyday. It wasn't a&nbsp;lot, having the whole garden for herself for an hour or a&nbsp;half, but it was still noticeably better than before. That's also when Lisa started getting more dog kibble to eat. Since I&nbsp;was still like 13 or 14 I&nbsp;couldn't do much more for her back then, but over time I&nbsp;always did my best to improve her living conditions further and further. Near my family house there is a&nbsp;small mountain and a&nbsp;road up it with a&nbsp;small bridge over a&nbsp;river. Only a&nbsp;few people live up the road so for most of the time that whole area was pretty empty: that's the road that became a&nbsp;path for my and Lisa's walks (we obviously never got close to the top of that mountain, even tho it's small). During one of those walks, while we were getting back, a&nbsp;snake came out of nowhere. I&nbsp;was scared like anyone would expect of me, but Lisa just scared the snake away. I&nbsp;was taking care of her and in return she was taking care of me... but it's not quite accurate, as she would try to take care of anyone in my family. One time when my parents also came along because they wanted to drag me further up the mountain a&nbsp;different dog was being pretty aggressive towards us all, but Lisa kept them at a&nbsp;distance. <br>
After some time, I&nbsp;did manage to have her have the garden opened at all times, so she could wander around and run around as she pleased. She got what she deserved all this time and was happy all the time. One time she did somehow break her leg, but she got all the support she needed and recovered pretty quickly^^. <br>
Lisa is also the one who would share various memories with me. She is also the one who was the first or one of the first to learn I&nbsp;had doubts about myself, then she was probably one of the first who did hear me accept myself as trans. She learned it all years before my family, and contrary to my family, she didn't seem to mind me being trans in the slightest. <br>
A few years ago, however, Lisa was diagnosed with breast cancer. She had it in a&nbsp;few of her "boobs" (for the lack of a&nbsp;better word). The decision was up to me then: would I&nbsp;rather have her have a&nbsp;surgery or live with it for as long as she can. I&nbsp;decided against the surgery, as Lisa was still happy and didn't have any problems and the surgery had a&nbsp;high chance she would not wake up after it. She is still happy to this day, making me not regret my choice from then, but with her being sick and old, it is no surprise her health is slowly getting worse. Funnily enough, most of the pictures of her are from the more recent times, since she slowed down a&nbsp;bit. Earlier she was so energetic that it was almost impossible to capture her on any picture ever^^. <br>
All in all, this is why I'm here now, writing about her. I&nbsp;want her to be remembered for the life she lived and things she'd done. I&nbsp;want her legacy to be written at a&nbsp;time when she is still quite happy, rather than a&nbsp;time when she is gone. Her legacy is meant to be made of cheerful memories rather than mourning. Courageous, strong, smart and caring, Lisa truly is a&nbsp;good girl and a&nbsp;fox-dog.
</p>
